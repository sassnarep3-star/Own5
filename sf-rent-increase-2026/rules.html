Legal framework

What is a normal and lawful increase?

For a rent-controlled San Francisco unit, the Rent Board's allowable annual increase—not a neighborhood market survey or a landlord's costs—is the routine annual benchmark. It is a ceiling for the relevant effective-date period, not a required increase and not automatically available in every case.

Verified current local rate

1.6% for annual rent increases effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027. The scheduled rate starting March 1, 2027 is not established by this current-rate source as of November 6, 2026. Apply only after confirming unit coverage, current lawful base rent, anniversary/12-month timing, license, notice, and any prior increases.

Eligibility: a 1940 build year is an important clue, not the full legal test

SF says many units built on or before June 13, 1979 have both rent control and eviction protection, while certain single-family homes/condos, substantially rehabilitated units, subsidized housing, and other categories can have partial or complete exceptions. The City says to check the exact building/unit and consult the Rent Board if classification is unclear. The year 1940 is owner-reported here, not independently verified.

For an exact answer, use the City's Property Information Map and ask the Rent Board about the specific unit/exception. A two-unit building and each unit's ownership / property form can matter. Do not assume that an owner description of “rent controlled” resolves every rule.

Ordinary annual increase vs. an above-allowance increase

Annual allowable increase

Routine local amount is determined by the Rent Board rate effective on the date of increase. The landlord need not prove that the annual increase matches particular taxes, insurance, maintenance expenses, or inflation. It is optional to take less or none. Timing, base rent and license rules still apply.

Annual increase rules ↗

Above the annual amount

A larger increase is not justified simply by saying “costs went up.” Certain expense/repair/capital-improvement grounds require a proper Rent Board petition, eligible costs, documentation, applicable allocation/limits, and in some cases a decision before notice or collection. Ordinary repair and maintenance is not automatically pass-through eligible.

Normal does not mean “market-rate”

For a continuing rent-controlled tenancy, comparing current asking rents nearby does not replace the Rent Board formula. Do not use a market reset just because rent appears below market; the vacancy/occupant rules are fact-specific and cannot be decided from this summary.
