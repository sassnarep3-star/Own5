Fair, consistent and reviewable

Business practice, fairness, and tenant review

No policy can guarantee that a rent increase will survive every dispute. A defensible process makes the law and the underlying records visible, uses neutral criteria, and avoids treating “reasonable” as a substitute for compliance.

Large real-estate firms vs. individual owners

What is verifiable

Official San Francisco, California and federal sources set legal rules; the sources reviewed do not establish how large corporations versus individual property owners typically set increases or what each group commonly does. It would be speculation to present anecdotes as verified industry practice. The applicable local rent ordinance does not provide a higher annual allowance because an owner is a corporation.

Prudent manager-style controls

  • Maintain a written rent ledger and keep rent notices, proof of service, leases and addenda.
  • Use a per-unit calendar based on verified effective dates—not a universal annual date.
  • Have a second reviewer check coverage, rent base, anniversary, notice days, license and arithmetic.
  • Separate base rent from temporary passthroughs and temporary improvement charges.
  • Document the same neutral decision criteria across tenancies and record exceptions.

These are recommended controls, not claims about what real-estate corporations actually do.

Prudent individual-owner controls

  • Do not rely on memory or the first lease rent alone; reconstruct written notices and receipts.
  • Use official Rent Board forms/instructions and ask the Board about rule questions.
  • Do not make a rent change to offset repair work, tenant complaints, roommate changes or protected activity. Federal anti-interference rule ↗
  • Get tenant-side counsel to review any uncertain rent-history, occupancy, vacancy-reset or pass-through issue.
  • Keep private tenant documents off this public site and share only with counsel / the Board as needed.

This checklist is practical guidance, not an empirical comparison.

Does it make sense to raise both units?

Not as a way to avoid a lawsuit or discrimination inquiry. No official authority identified provides a “raise both” safe harbor. One unit's lawful increase does not validate another unit's amount, and different rent histories can lawfully lead to different effective dates or dollar amounts.

Use a consistent policy for both: increase only when legally due, calculate each independently, give the same quality of notice, and apply the same nondiscriminatory criteria. If there is a neutral reason for a different decision (e.g., an unresolved date or a pending petition), record it. Do not take adverse action because of a protected characteristic or because a tenant asserted legal rights. Different treatment of similarly situated tenants can create scrutiny; a side-by-side comparison should be reviewed by a qualified housing attorney.

How a tenant-friendly advocate or lawyer could review it

1

Coverage and legal rent

Confirm unit-specific rent control/AB 1482 status, exact lawful base rent and the full notice/payment history.

2

Timing and arithmetic

Recalculate the local rate for the effective date; identify any properly banked increase; verify the 12-month rule and separate any pass-throughs.

3

License, petitions and service

Check license on the effective date; petition approval and filing order where required; notice amount, percent, effective date, service method, and proof.

4

Fair housing / retaliation

Compare neutral criteria for both households, look for inconsistent exceptions or protected-activity timing, and confirm no accommodation or protected-class issue is implicated.

5

Plain-language tenant notice

Explain the applicable annual vs. banked vs. petitioned components accurately; do not make unsupported claims that the increase is inevitable or “required by costs.”

Tenant-side assistance: what can be said accurately?

San Francisco's Rent Board offers free counseling on the Rent Ordinance and publishes referrals to tenant organizations and legal-service providers. Some referral entries describe free counseling or free legal clinics; others specify proof-of-income, location, age/disability, language, or case-type limits. The City does not guarantee a tenant a free attorney for every routine rent-increase notice, and Rent Board counselors are not a substitute for attorney representation.

If a tenant requests independent review, do not discourage it or suggest they must waive rights. Provide a clear written calculation and keep communication non-coercive. To seek the right service, see the current Rent Board Referral Listing ↗ and Rent Board counseling information ↗.
