termplain_english_definitionhow_it_is_computed_here
Own lineA point spread or total we calculate ourselves from official results, instead of copying a sportsbook's number.Ridge regression of home margin on team indicators, fitted only on weeks before the game being predicted.
Ridge regressionA way of fitting a model that stops extreme team ratings running away when a club has played only a few games.Solved in closed form with a penalty of 5.0 on the size of every team rating.
Home marginHome team's points minus the away team's points. Positive means the home team won.Straight from the official final scores.
Walk-forwardPredicting each week using only the weeks before it, so the test can never see the future.Week N is predicted from weeks 1 to N-1 only.
Out-of-sampleA prediction made on data the model never saw while it was being fitted.Every row of backtest_walkforward.csv.
Neutral-site gameA game played abroad, where neither club has a home crowd.Marked from the official nfl.com weekly schedule pages; home-field advantage is set to zero for these games.
Home-field advantage (HFA)How many points the home team is worth before you even look at who is playing.Mean home margin across non-neutral games, reported with a 95 per cent confidence interval.
MAEMean absolute error: on average, how many points a prediction missed by.Average of the absolute margin errors, compared against the naive alternative of predicting a zero margin every game.
BiasWhether the misses lean one way. Positive means the model under-rated the home team on average.Simple mean of the margin errors.
Break-even hit rateThe share of bets you must win just to cover the book's commission.At -110 you risk 1.00 to win 0.909, so break-even is 1.10 / 2.10 = 52.38 per cent.
Wilson intervalA range for a hit rate that behaves properly on small samples.Wilson score interval at 95 per cent.
Holm-BonferroniA correction applied when several signals are tested at once, because testing many signals manufactures winners.Step-down adjustment across the skill signals only; the bias probe is excluded.
Deflated Sharpe RatioA Sharpe ratio adjusted for how many strategies were tried and for lopsided returns. It asks: would this still look good if I had only ever tried one thing?Bailey and Lopez de Prado (2014), using the measured skew and excess kurtosis and a trials count equal to the number of signals tested.
Skew and excess kurtosisHow lopsided and how fat-tailed the per-bet returns are. Both make a good-looking record less trustworthy.Computed directly on the per-bet return series: +0.909 for a win, -1.000 for a loss.
Deficiency pointsHow far down a club's published depth chart a position has to reach. Zero means the first string starts; two means the third string starts.Walk the club's published depth order and skip any player the official injury report lists as Out.
String usedWhich line of the depth chart is expected to start: 1 means first string, 2 means second string and so on.Position of the expected starter in the club's published order.
Closing line valueHow much better the price you took was than the price available when the game started.NOT COMPUTED. It needs a market price and no official source publishes one (flag F-01).
UnitsBet size expressed as multiples of your standard stake.One unit risked: a win at -110 returns +0.909 units, a loss returns -1.000.
Correlation (Pearson r)How much two numbers move together, from -1 to +1.Standard Pearson correlation with a Fisher z confidence interval.
Same-game parlaySeveral bets on one game combined into a single ticket. The legs are not independent.Not priced here; the inputs you would need are the correlations in correlations.csv plus real parlay prices from a book.
Verified and unverifiedWhether a number was read off an official source or not.Every row carries its source URL. Anything without an official source is left blank and given a flag.
