Q1What is a reasonable and normal rental increase in SF 94122 under local, state, and federal law?For these rent-controlled (1940 building) units the LOCAL cap controls: the annual allowable increase set by the SF Rent Board — 1.6% for effective dates 3/1/2026–2/28/2027, 1.4% for 3/1/2025–2/28/2026 — applied once per 12 months on the anniversary date. 'Normal' practice is exactly this published CPI-linked annual adjustment. The STATE AB 1482 ceiling (8.8% for the SF region, 8/1/2026–7/31/2027) does not apply because SF's local ordinance is more restrictive. FEDERAL law sets no rent cap; it prohibits discrimination. Q2How do big real estate corporations vs individual owners handle increases in SF?The Rent Ordinance applies the SAME caps, notice, anniversary, banking, and license rules to every owner — the official sources reviewed draw no distinction between corporate and individual landlords for rent-controlled units. (AB 1482 does distinguish corporate ownership only for single-family/condo exemptions, irrelevant here.) Q3What is the correct way to inform tenants of a rent increase?Written notice at least 30 days before the effective date (90 days if the increase, alone or combined with another in the same year, exceeds 10%; +5 days if mailed). Notice should state the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and any banked portion; use the rate in effect on the effective date. The owner must hold a current rent increase license (Housing Inventory reporting) before the increase can take effect. No increase during a fixed-term lease unless the lease allows it.S01 ↗ S16 ↗ S05 ↗ S09 ↗ S15 ↗
Q4What are reasonable reasons for a rent increase?The annual allowable increase itself requires no additional justification — it is the lawful standard adjustment. Anything beyond it requires a Rent Board petition/passthrough (capital improvements, operating & maintenance, utility/bond passthroughs, comparable-rent special circumstances). Charging more because a roommate moved in is expressly unlawful; increases cannot be rounded up.S01 ↗ S17 ↗
Q5Can the increase pass review by an SF tenant lawyer / free tenant legal services?Yes if it stays within the verified rules above: correct rate for the effective date, 12-month spacing, valid license, proper written notice, base-rent-only math, correct handling of any banked amounts. Free help exists in SF: Eviction Defense Collaborative 415-659-9184 (free legal help for tenants), Rent Board counseling 415-252-4600, and the SF Human Rights Commission for discrimination/retaliation. This site is not legal advice and cannot substitute for attorney review — see F11.S07 ↗ S08 ↗ S01 ↗
Q6Raise one unit's rent or both — discrimination risk?No law requires raising both units, and raising both is not a 'safe harbor'. The defensible approach is a uniform, documented policy: apply the same lawful annual allowable increase to every covered unit on its own anniversary date, with identical notice procedures. Selectively targeting one household because of a protected class (race, color, religion, sex, familial status, national origin, disability) or in retaliation is unlawful under the federal Fair Housing Act; discrimination complaints in SF go to the Human Rights Commission. Evaluate each tenancy's lawful rent history independently.S13 ↗ S14 ↗ S08 ↗ S01 ↗
