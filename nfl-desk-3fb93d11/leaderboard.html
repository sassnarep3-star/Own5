Why the board is split

A leaderboard with one score lets a lucky staking run promote a signal that has no evidence behind it. So each family is scored twice, from disjoint inputs:

  • signal_score — how much the hit rate exceeds the break-even rate after shrinkage, the deflated Sharpe ratio against the number of families tried, the sample size, and whether it survives a Holm correction in the right direction. A family that is significantly worse than break-even is penalised, not rewarded.
  • staking_score — realised money only: flat-stake return plus a bounded Sharpe term. This is the axis a lucky run can win.

Only the signal axis can certify or reward anything. And ranking happens inside a board, never across universes: the certification board reads official results, the research board reads the mirror and has no standing.

The case the split was built for

On the certification board, first place on realised money is Fade large home favourite >= 10 with 4 graded bets and 43.182% return — and it sits 5 of 8 on signal. Under a single-score board that run would have been rewarded. Here it is labelled staking-only and cannot be certified.
