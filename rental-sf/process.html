B. Step-by-step service procedure (do these in order)

  1. Confirm coverage & build the file. Pull certificate of occupancy (SF Property Information Map ↗), lease(s), all prior notices/rent history. Call a Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) to confirm the unit’s status and your banking ledger.
  2. Housing Inventory → license. Report the units into the Rent Board Housing Inventory portal; the report generates the rent increase license. Imposing an annual/banked increase without a license = null and void (§37.15). Housing Inventory FAQ ↗
  3. Compute exactly. current base rent × rate(s) in effect on the effective date. Keep cents (e.g., $63.92). Show the banked years line by line. Never round up. Increase may not include passthroughs.
  4. Check the 180-day window. If any tenant gave a repair complaint/agency filing/judicial filing within 180 days, stop — CC §1942.5 presumes retaliation.
  5. Check the combined 12-month window. Sum every rent increase effective in the trailing + forward 12 months. ≤10% → 30 days; >10% → 90 days; +5 days if mailed. CC §827 ↗
  6. Serve the written notice. Contents: tenant name(s) (as on lease), unit, current base rent, dollar amount, percentage, effective date, banked itemization, landlord signature. Serve personally, or by mail (add 5 days) per CCP §1013. Posting alone is not enough for a unilateral increase.
  7. Proof & follow-through. Keep the signed notice, service proof (declaration/mail receipt), the computation sheet, the license confirmation. Charge only from the effective date. Calendar next anniversary.
  8. If above allowable: petition first. File with the Rent Board (capital/OM) → decision → then notice stating the certified amount. Hardship applications from tenants can suspend capital passthroughs.

D. Common failure modes (what tenant-side lawyers attack first)

  • Increase takes effect without a license in place for that effective date (§37.15 — void).
  • Not riding the correct anniversary math; jumping dates and “losing” banked amounts by mistake.
  • Rounding “$63.92” to “$65” — unlawful rounding-up.
  • Notice served 30 days ahead for an 11% combined increase (needed 90).
  • Banked ledger that compounds (“1.4% + 1.6% of the new total”) instead of adding and multiplying the current base.
  • Notice mentions only a percentage but never the dollar amount — facially defective.
  • Serving one unit faster/higher after that household complained to DBI/Rent Board — §1942.5 presumption.
  • Charging the surcharge for “existing pet/added roommate” — unlawful.
