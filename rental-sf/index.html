Read this first. For continuing, covered tenancies in San Francisco, the “normal and reasonable” increase is the City’s annual allowable increase plus any properly banked increases — never market repricing. Anything above that requires Rent Board certification (e.g., capital improvements), a vacancy (new tenancy), or fails review. SF Rent Board — Learn about rent increases ↗

Key findings

  • Local law is the ceiling. SF Rent Ordinance (pre-1979 buildings) sets the pace: 1.6% (2026-03-01→2027-02-28), once per 12 months, base rent only, no rounding up. Banking is additive, never compounded; a rent increase license (Housing Inventory) must be in effect or the increase is void (§37.15). sf.gov ↗
  • State law backstops, it doesn’t override. AB 1482’s 5% + SF-Oakland-Hayward CPI-U (April 2026: 3.8% → 8.8% for 2026-08-01→2027-07-31, < 10%) is displaced by SF’s stricter cap (§1947.12(d)(3)). CC §1947.12 ↗ · BLS CPI-SF ↗
  • Federal law sets no cap. The Fair Housing Act forbids discriminatory terms/conditions of rental (race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability); it does not limit amounts. HUD ↗
  • Unit 1 (Household 1): plain annual 1.6% = +$63.92 → $4,058.92. The skipped 2026-02-01 1.4% ($55.93) is likely lost if a new effective date is picked before 2027-02-01. Unit math ↗
  • Unit 2 (Household 2): annual only +$50.40 → $3,200.40; fully banked 2020–2026 skips could add up to +$412.65 → $3,562.65 (90-day notice; banking eligibility is officially ambiguous — flag F-02). Unit math ↗
  • One unit vs both: no law requires equal treatment, but formula-identical treatment of both units is the most defensible pattern; anything else shifts the burden of proof. Analysis ↗
  • Corporate vs individual owners: DOJ + 10 states (incl. CA) allege RealPage and six large landlords used shared competitors’ data to set renewal increases; individual SF owners face the same hard caps — the law does not care about owner size. Practice ↗ · DOJ ↗
  • Reasonable reasons are statutory: CPI-based annual increase, properly banked years, certified capital-improvement/OM passthroughs (petition first; never rolled into base rent) — nothing else survives tenant-side review. Process ↗
