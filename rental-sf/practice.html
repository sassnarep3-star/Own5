DOJ v. RealPage — what it does and doesn’t prove
QuestionAnswer
Who was sued?RealPage Inc. + six landlords (Greystar, LivCor, Camden, Cushman/Pinnacle, Willow Bridge, Cortland); 10 state AGs incl. California. DOJ ↗
What’s alleged?Algorithmic price-setting from shared non-public competitor data; suppression of competition & concessions in 1.3M+ units/43 states+DC. DOJ ↗
Is it adjudicated?No. Allegations unproven; RealPage disputes them; Cortland’s consent decree is “proposed” (pending court approval). Flag F-08.
Does it bind a 2-unit owner?No — but it signals the legal climate: coordination with competitors on rent levels is an antitrust problem, full stop. An owner pegged to competitors’ non-public rents inherits the theory; the SF formula is the safe harbor. DOJ ↗

What “normal business practice” looks like here (the defensible template)

  1. License current each March (Housing Inventory report filed).
  2. Same method for both units: annual allowable % + properly banked years, recomputed per unit.
  3. Written notice, exact cents, effective date = anniversary (or a deliberate new date with the banking consequence understood).
  4. No competitor data anywhere in the file; the file contains only sf.gov schedules + lease + prior notices.
  5. If above-allowable: petition first, decision in hand, then notice citing the certified amount.

“Normal business practice” is characterization/analysis, not an official metric — no official SF dataset measures increase amounts by owner type. Flag F-12.
