Federal law — does any of it cap the rent?

Short answer: No. No federal statute caps residential rent increases, and no federal agency sets rent levels in San Francisco. Federal law contributes (1) anti-discrimination duties for how rent is set and changed, and (2) antitrust limits on how landlords coordinate pricing.

2. Antitrust — how landlords may not coordinate increases

DOJ v. RealPage — the case every institutional landlord now prices against
ItemVerified fact (as of 2026-11-06)Source
SuitU.S. DOJ + AGs of CA, CO, CT, IL, MA, MN, NC, OR, TN, WA filed a civil Sherman Act (§§1–2) antitrust suit against RealPage Inc. — then an amended complaint (2025-01-07) adding six large landlords: Greystar, Blackstone/LivCor, Camden Property Trust, Cushman & Wakefield/Pinnacle, Willow Bridge (ex-Lincoln Residential), Cortland.DOJ release 25-21 ↗

3. What federal law does not do

  • No federal maximum percentage, no CPI formula, no caps by building age (that’s state/local).
  • No federal approval or license for rent increases.
  • No federal “right to a rent increase” for owners — caps are set downward by SF/CA.
  • No federal exemption that overrides SF’s license requirement.
Bottom line: the binding numbers for these units come from San Francisco (1.6% + banking) with California’s §827 notice rules; federal law disciplines how increases are decided and selected, never how much. Full state picture on the State law page.
