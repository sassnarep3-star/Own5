One unit, or both? The discrimination & retaliation lens

The brief asks whether raising one household’s rent (or both) is lawful and defensible under SF’s strict tenant protections — and whether it survives a tenant-side lawyer’s review. This page answers with the actual doctrine, not vibes.

Applying it to this building

  • Unit 1 (Household 1) — tenancy 2025-02-01, $3,995.00, anniversary ≈ Feb 1. A 1.6% notice now = +$63.92 → $4,058.92. Not “they” get more because Unit 2 has more banking; Unit 2’s larger band derives from time served, not identity.
  • Unit 2 (Household 2) — tenancy 2019-06-01, $3,150.00, anniversary June 1. Current-year 1.6% = +$50.40 → $3,200.40; banked scenario = larger bump — again, time served, formula, identical method.
  • Identical method, different outputs is the architecture that defeats a disparate-treatment theory: same formula, same sequence, same notice, same service, same proof — outputs differ only because inputs (anniversary dates, base rents, banked years) differ.
  • Household 1 went from 3 to 2 occupants (2026-04-01). This changes nothing lawfully: fewer occupants is not a reason to raise/lower rent; “additional occupant” surcharges are unlawful; the only thing occupancy affects is who must be named on the notice.
  • 180-day sweep: before noticing either unit, confirm no tenant made a habitability/agency/court filing in the trailing 180 days. If yes → pause, consult counsel.

The tenant-side lawyer’s checklist (what must be true for “reasonable business practice”)

  1. Certificate of occupancy & construction record reviewed; coverage confirmed in writing. Coverage ↗
  2. License in effect on the effective date (§37.15).
  3. Anniversary correct; once per 12 months; no rounding up; exact cents.
  4. Every banked year traced to the official schedule; chosen date doctrine documented (learned-page read vs banked-page read). F-02
  5. Notice: dollar + % + effective date + banked itemization; 30/90 days; +5 if mailed; service proof.
  6. 180-day retaliation sweep documented clean (or notice deferred).
  7. Both units: same formula, same wording, same process; differences explained by dates/amounts only.
  8. No protected-class motive anywhere; no competitor-pricing data; only SF schedules & lease facts in the file.
  9. Capital passthroughs (if any): petition first, decision before notice, hardship process disclosed.
  10. Tenant file kept; copy of notice + computation + license given/kept.
Answer to the brief, in one line: raise both units by their own lawful maxima using one identical formula — it is the only pattern that is simultaneously (i) lawful, (ii) uniform, (iii) discrimination-proof on its face, and (iv) review-passing by a tenant-side lawyer. Raising one unit is possible but strictly riskier and never “fairer by dollar amount”.
