Reading the two official banking passages (why this is ambiguous)

Passage 1 — “Banked rent increases” (sf.gov): “A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date. … Banking has only been allowed since April 1, 1982. … There is no limit on how much can be banked … Banked increases do not expire during a tenancy; new owners may use banked increases from prior owners.” sf.gov ↗
Passage 2 — “Learn about rent increases” (sf.gov), worked example: “A tenant moved in on June 1, 2023 … the owner wants to increase the tenant’s rent for the first time on June 1, 2026. The total amount of banked increases that could be imposed on June 1, 2025, is 3.1% (1.7% for June 1, 2024 + 1.4% for June 1, 2025 = 3.1%). The owner is also entitled to impose the annual allowable increase in effect on June 1, 2026 (1.6%). The total allowable rent increase is 4.7%.” → two consecutive skipped increases both bankable at the second anniversary; only a skip younger than ~12 full months at the new effective date is lost. sf.gov ↗
  • If Passage 2 governs → U2-C available ($3,562.65). If Passage 1 governs → U2-D ($3,465.00) as of 2026-04-01; U2-C reaches 13.1% only once every skipped year is ≥ 24 months stale with no intervening increase (effectively by 2027-06-01, and only if 2024/2025 skips are still intact then).
  • Resolution requires the Rent Board counselor’s reading of the actual tenancy file (any past fee/license/service changes count as “increases”?). Do not serve any banked combination without that ruling.
