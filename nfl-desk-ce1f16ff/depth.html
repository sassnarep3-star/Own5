Depth, as a measured quantity

Knowing that a starter was replaced tells a bettor almost nothing; knowing how deep the replacement is tells them nearly everything. So every position group on every published depth chart — 705 of them across 31 clubs — is graded against the replacement's own snaps in 2026: 90 TESTED, 423 PARTIALLY TESTED, 116 UNTESTED, 76 with no second name published at all.

Grading rules, written down first

ruledefinition
TESTEDsecond string has median snap share >= 0.5 of the unit's snaps in >= 3 games of 2026
PARTIALLY TESTEDsecond string appeared in >= 1 game but below the TESTED thresholds
UNTESTEDsecond string has no snaps in the 2026 play records (injury, healthy scratch, practice squad, or a name the two sources spell differently)
NO NAME PUBLISHEDthe club's depth chart lists no second string at that position
joinnames are matched exactly after normalisation; no fuzzy matching is used anywhere in this file
peer columndeficiency_points is another session's model output, published unchanged in its own column and never combined with the measured columns

The position the desk takes on UNTESTED

A second-stringer with 40 snaps in nine weeks is not a bad replacement. They are an unpriced one. Those are different claims, and the difference is the reason this page publishes a grade instead of a projection: an unpriced absence should make a line look uncertain, not make the model confident in either direction.
