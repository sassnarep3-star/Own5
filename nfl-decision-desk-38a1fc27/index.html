What the numbers say

Lineup loss predicts margin, not the market

A percentage point of relative lineup deficiency is worth about -0.039 points of margin (p = 6.07152e-05). But fading the more-injured team is only 51.4% ATS over 782 bets (p = 1). The market already prices most of it.

The market prices about half of the injury effect

Regressing the market spread on the same deficiency measure gives -0.019 points per percentage point, about 49% of the raw effect. The leftover -0.020 per point is statistically weak (p = 0.024) and produces no binary ATS edge, and the line's timestamp is unknown (F01).

Same-team prop legs are heavily correlated

QB passing yards and his WR1's receiving yards correlate at r = 0.50 (n = 1,255 team-games). At median lines the joint hit rate is 32.5% versus 24.8% if legs were independent — a +7.7 pp gap that changes the fair price.

Sign conventions are the top risk to this project

The first injury backtest reported a 57.4% ATS edge purely because the spread was used with the wrong sign. Fixed, the same test returns 51.4%. Every backtest now carries a convention note and the error is retained in the log.
