Executive summary

What was built, what is verified, what is missing, and the decisions that follow.

Objective

Originate our own NFL lines, test strategies that might carry skill, price and understand correlation across singles, parlays and player-prop combinations, and track injuries and lineup depth well enough to know when a starter is replaced by a 2nd, 3rd or 4th string player — with every value traceable to an official source or explicitly flagged.

What is verified

  • Official data, row by row. 276 player rows from the official Week 10 injury report, the Week 10 schedule and the 2026 standings, each with a source link and a retrieval timestamp recorded in provenance.json.
  • Dual-source agreement. All 32 clubs' standings reproduce exactly from an independent game log; all 276 injury rows match the independent mirror.
  • Own lines. A spread and total for all 14 Week 10 games, fitted only on prior weeks, with the injury adjustment shown separately.

What is computed

  • Injury value. A percentage point of relative lineup deficiency is worth about -0.0391315 points of margin (p = 6.07152e-05). The market moves -0.0190095 points per point — roughly half the effect — leaving a residual of -0.020122 (p = 0.0242031).
  • No betting edge. Fading the more-injured team is 51.4% ATS over 782 bets, p = 1.0. The model's own walk-forward ATS record is 50.2-50.9%, i.e. no edge.
  • Correlation. QB passing yards and his WR1's receiving yards correlate at r = 0.50; at median lines the joint hit rate is 32.5% versus 24.8% if independent. Cross-market legs (spread vs total) are independent (phi = 0.006).
  • Zero certified strategies. 7 families tested against α = 0.00714; none passes every gate.

What is missing, and why it matters

  • No verified price history (F01, F02). Without a timestamped operator price, closing-line value cannot be computed and no strategy can be certified as skill. This is the single largest blocker; suggestion S01 addresses it.
  • No official prop lines (F03). Prop combinations are priced by correlation, not against real lines, so their economic value is unknown.
  • Depth is inferred (F05, F16). Snap share is a measurable proxy for a depth chart the league does not publish.
  • Prior-session figures do not all reproduce (F08-F10). The home-underdog, QB/WR correlation and 20.8% luck figures are flagged for human review.

The five decisions in front of you

  1. Capture a real price history (S01) — without it, nothing can ever be certified.
  2. Adopt the sign-convention regression test (S02) — it already caught one invented edge.
  3. Close the jurisdiction and funding questions (Q04, Q05) before publishing any reward amount.
  4. Approve the correction of the four non-reproducing prior figures (F08-F10), or ask for a second opinion first.
  5. Decide whether the injury metric is a pricing input only (recommended, S05) or a bet trigger (not supported by the data).

Bottom line

The desk is now automated, sourced and reproducible. The honest current state is: good infrastructure, verified inputs, no certified edge. That is a much better position than a leaderboard full of unverified win rates — and it is exactly what "remove luck from the equation" requires.

Verification policy. Every table row links to an official page or to the script and pinned input that produced it. Values that cannot be confirmed are left blank and published on the Flagged for review page — never inferred.
