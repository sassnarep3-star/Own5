What was done this session

  • Six official captures were read on 2026-11-15: league standings, the week-11 schedule, the week-10 results page, the week-10 injury report, a five-week spot-check of the results feed, and 31 of 32 club depth charts. Each is listed with its URL and capture time on the sources page.
  • The carried-forward results feed was re-checked against the official week pages: 59 of 59 re-read rows agreed, and the feed reconciles to the official standings for 32 of 32 clubs (results).
  • Injury designations were read twice, two days apart, and 4 rows were observed to change between the two official reads — direct evidence that the report is a series, not a weekly snapshot (injuries).
  • 39 markets were originated for week 11 in the required order — side, then total, then moneyline — from a rating model fitted on 145 official margins (originated lines).
  • Every depth slot was transcribed, replacements named, and a 563-row offence-versus-defence deficiency matrix built for the week-11 slate (matchup matrix).

The three findings that matter

1. There is no price data, so nothing can be graded. nfl.com publishes no spreads, totals or moneylines, and no operator price has been read into this repository. Of 52 price-snapshot rows this desk needs, only 13 could be filled — and those are our own model outputs, not market prices. ROI, closing line value and the residual against the market are undefined, so the staking leaderboard is empty by design and no strategy can be certified.
2. Home-field advantage is not established in this sample. The model fits home field at -0.12 points per game with a 95% interval of -2.14 to 1.90 on 145 official games. The interval contains zero. Any strategy that leans on home field is leaning on a number this sample cannot support.
3. Neither totals estimator beats the naive baseline, and the first run that said otherwise was wrong. Fitted on prior weeks only, estimator A has a walk-forward mean absolute error of 11.61 points and estimator B 11.89, against a league-average baseline of 11.18. An earlier run of this build appeared to show estimator B winning by about a point; that was leakage, because it was fitted on the full-season standings, which contain the game being predicted (flag F-18). Both estimators stay published side by side with the gap column, as the brief asks, and neither is collapsed until the pre-registered comparison fires.
