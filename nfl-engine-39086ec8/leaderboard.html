5 · Signal and staking, separately

A lucky staking run cannot mask a bad signal because the signal column does not know what the stakes were.

The masking problem, in exact numbers

A synthetic strategy winning 5 of 20 bets (25%, far below the 52.38% break-even) ends with a bankroll +31.8% higher because its five largest stakes all won. Flat-staked it loses -10.45 units. Ranking on profit would have promoted it.

Why R3 is blocked despite a 75% hit rate

R3 selects the team with the better scoring margin using standings that include the games it is scored on. That is leakage, not skill, so the verdict column reads NOT_ELIGIBLE_IN_SAMPLE. A footnote would not stop that number being quoted later.

No entry has a verified closing-line value

Every verdict above is measured against a fixed −110 price, not against the price actually available at the time of bet. That is enough to score a signal consistently, and not enough to certify skill (requirement 8, rule D2).
