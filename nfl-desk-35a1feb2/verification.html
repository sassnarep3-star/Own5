Method

  • Read then re-derive. Raw transcript → CSV → page. The site is never hand-edited; engine.py regenerates every derived table and verify.py rebuilds it and compares bytes.
  • Row-level provenance. verify.py walks the generated HTML: every body row must end in an official link, a labelled third-party link, or an UNVERIFIED badge.
  • Transcript containment. Every number published in the standings and results tables must appear in the raw transcript, so a table cannot quietly gain a value the read did not contain.
  • Capture integrity. SHA-256 of every raw file and of each carried-forward input is recorded in the manifest and re-checked on every build.
  • Cross-source reconciliation. Standings against the game-by-game log, club by club — the check that found the club-code defect.
  • Unit tests. The scorer, the correlation interval, the play-rate universe rule, the alias map, and the no-invention rules are tested directly.

Limits of this verification

The hash proves the transcript was not edited after the read, not that the league served those bytes. The fetcher, not curl, is the only channel that reaches the league's site from this sandbox. Third-party figures come from in-repo mirrors of aggregator releases; they are labelled, kept out of the model, and out of the leaderboard. Nothing here is a claim that any strategy has an edge.
