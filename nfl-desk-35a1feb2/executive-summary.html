Executive summary

Written only from the tables on this desk; every number here appears in a table with a source.

1. The state of the project in one paragraph

The machinery to originate NFL lines exists, is deterministic, is sourced, and ran today: 149 verified games feed a frozen ridge model that published 39 pre-kickoff forecasts for the 13 Week 11 fixtures across side, total and moneyline in that order. What does not exist is the other half of the loop. There is no admitted market price, so no residual; one season of official results, so no certified sample; no verified depth for 32 of 32 clubs, so no replacement-quality feature; and no scheduled capture writing history, so no replayable availability series. Nine validation gates: two pass. Eight registered strategy families: zero certified. That is not caution, it is the count.

2. What today's read changed

  • The verified record grew from 145 to 149 games and now reconciles with the standings page for all 32 clubs — after fixing a club-code defect (LAR/ARI against LA/AZ) that had been silently dropping two clubs' games in any join.
  • Home-field advantage is not what the conventions assume: over 149 verified finals the mean home margin is −0.51 points (−0.26 excluding eight neutral-site games), home teams won 47.65%. A +2.5 anchor would misprice every side this season.
  • Designation levels carry almost no information on their own, but designation changes are rare too: two reads of the same weekly report 1 hour 13 minutes apart differ by nothing across 147 field values. The informative artefacts are the report's publication days, the roster register, and the inactive list nobody is capturing.
  • The reserve list settles why a player is missing from a report (e.g. a quarterback on the injured reserve list since 11/10 for Miami) and unsettles a claim repeated across desks about Baltimore's quarterbacks, for which the register carries no move at all.
  • Roster turnover is not decoration: 13 of 62 dated roster events contradict a published depth row, including a receiver placed on reserve on 10/28 still listed first-string by his club.

3. The one finding that changes the plan's shape

The brief says a price-data policy must be decided before any backtest is published, and an earlier flag on this site says no market price of any kind exists in the repo. Both are load-bearing and the second is wrong: a hashed multi-season log in this repo carries a spread and total on 7,439 of 7,548 games and moneylines on 5,458 (163 of 272 games in 2026). So the blocker is a decision about source class, not a data hunt — one paragraph of policy, written into the repo, converts several “impossible this season” gates into “computable tomorrow”. It also decides whether the 136-game snap corpus (identified: 12,693 rows across exactly 136 games, weeks 1–9) can support the playing-time depth model the brief asks for. Nothing on this desk is labelled a backtest, and no backtest number appears on any leaderboard, until that decision exists.

4. What the model is and is not

Ridge team-strength on game margins, λ=1.0 on team ratings, home term estimated, trained only on games finished before the recorded forecast time, residual sd 10.4 points, fitted home term −0.46. Two total estimators are published side by side with their gap because neither retires: A wins weeks 5, 7, 9; B wins 6, 8, 10 — and the naive league-average total beat both in weeks 7 and 9 (MAE 9.38 and 10.99 against 10.39/11.23 and 11.03/11.11). That is the honest scoreboard, including the part where the model loses to a constant.

5. What the leaderboard currently says

Nothing, by construction. Eight families are registered with frozen rule text, a two-sided falsifier each, and 39 recorded forecasts. Every score cell is blank because Week 11 has not been played, and the promotion rule says a signal must beat a coin flip, a best-differential pick and a league-average total before it is reported as anything. Signal and staking are separate tables so a lucky stake run cannot lift a bad signal.

7. Standing rejections (kept, because they are the reason the numbers are trustworthy)

  • Deriving depth order from jersey number, experience or salary — rejected by the brief and by this desk.
  • Ranking strategies on season-long ROI — rejected; 149 games cannot separate 53% from 50%.
  • Parlays and prop combos without a validated joint probability and the operator's void rules — withheld.
  • Fixed point penalties for missing starters — not applied; there is no availability feature in the model at all.
  • Filling a blank cell because a plausible number exists — refused at build time by verify.py.
