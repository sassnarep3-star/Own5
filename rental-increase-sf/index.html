Bottom line

For covered SF rent-controlled tenancies, the annual allowable increase is 1.6% for effective dates March 1, 2026–February 28, 2027. This is a legal ceiling for the annual component, not a market-based target. Do not serve a notice until coverage, tenant anniversary, base rent, license, and banked-increase ledger are confirmed.

Executive summary

Building age (1940) is consistent with the City’s general description of older rent-controlled housing, but age alone does not establish that a particular unit is covered or identify every exemption. Confirm each unit’s legal status through the Rent Board / lease records. The City says annual and banked increases require a rent-increase license and Housing Inventory reporting; tenants generally receive 30 days’ written notice, or 90 days when the increase exceeds 10%, plus five days when mailed.

Fairness: Apply the same neutral, documented method to similarly situated units, but do not increase both units merely to avoid a discrimination claim. A lawful increase on one unit is not automatically discriminatory; a blanket increase on both is not a legal safe harbor. Keep objective records, apply lawful timing and amounts, and obtain tenant-lawyer review if facts differ or protected-class / retaliation concerns exist.

Normal practice: Official rules establish limits and procedure, not what owners commonly do. There is no reliable official source here establishing a “normal” owner or corporate practice, nor a market-comparable increase for 94122. Treat those claims as unverified; do not use them to set an amount.

Practical checklist

  1. Confirm rent-control coverage and current lawful base rent for each unit.
  2. Confirm the last increase / tenancy anniversary and that at least 12 months have elapsed.
  3. Check the 1.6% annual rate in force on the proposed effective date; separately audit any banked amounts and applicable notice rules.
  4. Obtain the required rent-increase license / confirm Housing Inventory reporting.
  5. Give a written notice specifying dollar amount, percentage, and effective date; allow the required lead time.
  6. Retain the calculation, license, delivery proof, lease history, and a consistent non-discriminatory rationale.
