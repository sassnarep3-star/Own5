Removing Luck: Skill-vs-Variance Separation

Betting outcomes are variance-dominated on human horizons. "Removing luck" means grading on metrics whose expectation reflects skill and whose noise shrinks fast. Repo detail: research/skill-vs-luck.md.

1 · Grade on de-vigged CLV, not results

3 · Shrinkage scoring

Leaderboard skill = sample mean CLV pulled toward 0 in proportion to noise (few bets ⇒ heavy pull). Climbing the board on a short lucky run is mathematically unattractive — the anti-luck mechanism lives in the scoring rule.

4 · Stake standardization & Kelly constraint

Flat-stake (1-unit) performance is computed alongside actual staking so one lucky max-bet can't dominate. Staking is capped at fractional Kelly — half Kelly keeps ≈75% of growth at a fraction of the variance (marketmath INDUSTRY; Kelly 1956 via oddscipher PRIMARY).

5 · "Real effect" ≠ "market misses it"

B2B fatigue is real (44% vs 50%, arXiv:2606.27957 PRIMARY) — but it's only an edge if the closing line underprices it. All strategies are evaluated on residual power versus the closing line, which removes the largest class of false systems.

6 · Falsification required

Each pre-registered spec must state its own kill criteria (e.g., "mean CLV < 0 over any rolling 300-bet window ⇒ retire"). Unfalsifiable systems are not eligible.
