## Why the units are (almost certainly) rent controlled — and what controls the math

- The San Francisco Rent Ordinance covers residential units issued a certificate of occupancy on or
  before **June 13, 1979**, with narrow exceptions (short-term hotel rooms, non-profit co-ops,
  hospitals/dorms, government-regulated units, post-1979 construction, substantial rehabilitation,
  most single-family homes/condos) — [Learn about San Francisco rental laws, sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws). A duplex-style
  two-unit building built 1940 is the paradigm covered case; a "single-family dwelling with a legal
  in-law unit constitutes a two-unit building and is **not** exempt" — [Costa-Hawkins partial-exemption guidance, sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/information--partial-exemption-certain-single-family-homes-and-condominiums-under-costa-hawkins).
- California's statewide cap (AB 1482, Civil Code §1947.12) yields to a stricter local rent-control
  ordinance — i.e., the SF cap controls — [Civil Code §1947.12(d)(3), leginfo.legislature.ca.gov](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1947.12.&lawCode=CIV).
- Both units are continuing tenancies: the landlord may **not** reset rent to market. Market-rate
  initial rents are only for genuinely new tenancies (Costa-Hawkins vacancy decontrol) — [Civil Code §1954.53, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1954.53.&lawCode=CIV).

**Coverage is an assumption pending verification** (building permit/certificate of occupancy, current
owner records, Rent Board counselor confirmation). See flag **F-01**.

## Bottom-line answers (details, formulas, and flags on the site)

1. **"Reasonable and normal" for these continuing, covered tenancies = the annual allowable
   increase (1.6% for increases effective 2026-03-01→2027-02-28) plus any properly banked increases,
   imposed once every 12 months, after a rent increase license is in effect, with proper notice.**
   Anything larger needs Rent Board certification or waits for vacancy (new tenancy). Federal law
   imposes **no** rent cap. The state backstop (8.8% for 2026-07-31 window; Civil Code §1947.12) is
   displaced by the stricter local ordinance. See `rental-sf/index.html`.
2. **Unit 1 (Household 1), plain annual increase effective now:** $3,995.00 × 1.6% = **+$63.92 → $4,058.92**.
   The skipped 1.4% first-anniversary increase is probably no longer available as a new-anniversary
   increase (only ~2 months elapsed; increases move anniversary dates and un-banked amounts are lost).
3. **Unit 2 (Household 2), plain annual increase:** $3,150.00 × 1.6% = **+$50.40 → $3,200.40**.
   Fully banking every skipped year 2020–2026 would give up to **+$412.65 → $3,562.65**
   (13.1% combined), which requires **90-day** notice (>10% combined) and counselor sign-off — flag F-02.
4. **One unit or both:** nothing in local/state/federal law forbids raising both units by their
   own lawful maxima, or raising one and not the other, so long as the method is lawful, not
   retaliatory (180-day presumption, Civil Code §1942.5), and not based on a protected
   characteristic (federal Fair Housing Act; California FEHA). The lowest-risk, most defensible
   pattern — and the one most likely to satisfy tenant-side counsel review — is **the same lawful
   formula applied uniformly to both units**, with written, formula-based notices. Selective
   single-unit increases invite comparative scrutiny; see `rental-sf/fair-housing.html`.
5. **Do not propose or serve anything yet.** Verify coverage (flag F-01), obtain/confirm the rent
   increase license, have a Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) and a tenant-side-sounding
   qualified attorney review the notices, then serve 30- or 90-day written notices (5 extra days if mailed).
