Uniform, formula-based application across the two units; no protected-class or source-of-income targeting — [HUD FHA Overview](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview); [CRD Fair Housing Fact Sheet (PDF)](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2022/11/Fair-Housing-Fact-Sheet_ENG.pdf).

## 4. How big corporations (and individual owners) actually handle increases

- **Institutional/corporate owners:** algorithmic revenue management. The DOJ + 10 state attorneys general (incl. California) allege RealPage and six large landlords (Greystar, LivCor, Camden, Cushman/Pinnacle, Willow Bridge, Cortland) used shared, non-public competitors' data to coordinate increases and limit concessions — Sherman Act §§1–2 civil antitrust action, filed 2024-08-23; amended 2025-01-07; Cortland proposed consent decree; RealPage disputes the allegations; status unresolved. [DOJ press release 25-21 (archived, justice.gov)](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/justice-department-sues-six-large-landlords-algorithmic-pricing-scheme-harms-millions) CA is a co-plaintiff.
- **Both corporate and individual owners of SF-controlled units** in fact face the identical hard caps (1.6%/yr + banking + certified passthroughs); the law does not distinguish owner type. The difference is method: scheduled annual percentage increases, renewal-increase matrices, market surveys ("call-arounds" alleged by DOJ), versus a small owner who usually applies the CPI figure or banks it. **Normal, compliant corporate practice = the same annual allowable + banked framework above** — not market repricing of sitting tenants (that is only lawful at vacancy).
- Individual SF owners commonly deal with: banking multi-year skipped increases, Housing Inventory licensing, and capital-improvement passthroughs for big-ticket work. Individual owners do **not** have broader legal room to raise rent than corporations; SF applies identically.
- Note on "comparable units": the Rent Ordinance contemplates increases certified through Rent Board arbitration based on comparability (§37.3), which is how tribunals test whether an above-allowable increase is "reasonable" — not a free pass to market.

## 5. One unit or both? — the discrimination analysis

**Legal baseline.** Neither FHA nor FEHA nor the Rent Ordinance prohibits lawful, uniform, formula-based increases in one unit only. But disparity raises the burden of proof in a tenant challenge:

| Approach | Legal status | Risk level (assessment, not advice) |
|---|---|---|
| Apply identical lawful formula (annual + any valid banked share) to **both** units, same effective date structure, same notice wording | Lawful; textbook uniform practice; strongest defense to discrimination claims & "why me?" inquiries | **Lowest** — recommended pattern |
| Raise one unit; lawful cap, valid rationale, formula-based, timing clean (not within 180 days of tenant action) | Lawful in principle | Moderate — invites comparison & counsel scrutiny; must be airtight on banking/license/timing |
| Raise one unit disproportionately or without caps/license/notice, or shortly after a tenant complaint | Unlawful or presumptively retaliatory | **High** — fails any competent tenant-counsel review |
| Different percentages for protected-class-correlated reasons (e.g., family status, source of income, disability) | Unlawful (FHA/FEHA/Unruh) | **Prohibited** |

Unit-specific facts matter: Unit 1 (2025 lease, $3,995) and Unit 2 (2019 lease, $3,150) have **different banked balances**. "Raising both by the same dollar amount" would exceed Unit 1's lawful maximum (and thus be void); "raising both by each unit's lawful maximum" is the uniform, defensible formulation. Practically: serve both households the same-method notices (their anniversaries differ — Unit 1 ≈ Feb 1, Unit 2 = June 1 — which is the lawful, formula-driven reason numbers and dates differ, not person-specific treatment).

**Recommended pattern (informational; not legal advice):** uniform method for both; license current; identical notice template with unit-specific computed figures; independent review of the math by Rent Board counsel + qualified attorney; keep the paper file. If the owner only raises one now, the file must document a neutral, lawful reason that has nothing to do with the household's identity — otherwise don't.

## 6. Correct process & reasonable reasons (the "how to do it" checklist)

Reasonable (lawful) reasons for an increase over sitting tenancies in SF, all source-linked:
1. Annual allowable increase (CPI-based; 1.6% for 2026-03-01→2027-02-28) — [sf.gov announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced).
2. Banked increases (skipped prior years; additive, never compounded; unmatured years are lost) — [sf.gov banked increases](https://sf.gov/information/banked-rent-increases).
3. Certified capital improvement passthrough (petition first; 100% of certified cost; amortized; not base rent; discontinue at end) — [sf.gov capital improvement petitions](https://www.sf.gov/information/capital-improvement-petitions).
4. Other certified Rent Board petitions (e.g., operating & maintenance increases, capped at 7%) — [Sec. 37.7 (sf.gov)](https://www.sf.gov/information/sec-377-certification-rent-increases-capital-improvements-rehabilitation-work-energy).
5. **Not** lawful: repricing to "market" for sitting tenants; >cap without certification; skipping notice; skipping the license; retaliatory timing; extra-roommate surcharges; rounding up; raising more than once per 12 months.

Steps (sf.gov "Learn about rent increases"):
1. Confirm coverage + pull the certificate of occupancy (Property Information Map) and Rent Board records; speak to a counselor (415-252-4600).
2. Confirm/file the Housing Inventory → rent increase license for each unit (annual, every Mar 1).
3. Compute base rent × rate(s) in effect on the planned effective date; never round up; itemize banked years.
4. Serve written notice: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, banked portion; 30 days if combined ≤10%, else 90 days; +5 days if mailed; keep proof of service.
5. File copies of everything; calendar the next anniversary; confirm the tenant is charged only on the effective date.
