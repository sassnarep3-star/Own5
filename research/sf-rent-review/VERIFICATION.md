## Controlling distinctions checked

- [SF Rent Board announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced) and [rate schedule](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit): **1.6%** for ordinary annual increases effective March 1, 2026–February 28, 2027. This is the ordinary allowance, not a survey of actual increases, not a universal total ceiling on all authorized components, and not a determination that either unit is eligible. Rates after February 2027 are not assumed.
- [Rent Board annual guidance](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco): use lawful base rent, not temporary passthroughs; do not round the increase up; first annual increase after at least 12 months; later lawful effective dates can change the anniversary; obtain a license. A landlord may use less than the allowance according to the [banking guidance](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases).
- [Banking](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases): a wholly skipped increase banks only when 24 full months pass without a rent increase; partial unused increases have their own rule; eligible rates are added, not compounded or prorated. A long tenancy or a reported unchanged rent does not itself establish a banked balance or eligibility. A simple ordinary-only adjustment is an assessment, not a claim that banking is illegal.
- [Coverage](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws) is not established solely by a reported construction year. No street address or private official records were available. [Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory): annual and banked increases require an effective current license; a license alone does not establish legal compliance.
- [SF § 37.3](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) restricts adding rent solely due to a new occupant, subject to statutory exceptions, and its subsection (d)(2)(B) addresses partial changes. [SF’s §6.14/Costa-Hawkins explanation](https://www.sf.gov/information--rent-increases-under-section-614-and-costa-hawkins) distinguishes original occupants, co-tenants and subtenants. A later signed document does not by itself establish a vacant new tenancy, a reset anniversary, or a lawful market increase. The requested status is owner-reported, not independently verified.
- [AB 1482](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482): 8.8% for applicable state-only units in SF in the stated 2026–27 period is **not** substituted for SF’s local limit. [HUD](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview) and [CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/HTML/IF12887.web.html) document federal fair housing and assistance-program restrictions, not an independently established universal federal numeric cap for these particular units.
- [Civil Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=827&lawCode=CIV) specifies service and thresholds for covered periodic tenancies. Mail extension under [CCP § 1013](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1013) depends on location; 35/95 days applies to in-state mail in ordinary circumstances. [DRE guidance](https://www.dre.ca.gov/publications/ResourceGuidebook/gb06_before.html) warns a fixed-term contract ordinarily must allow a midterm increase. Longer notice and program-specific rules may apply.
- [SF § 37.3(b)](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-373-rent-limitations) requires written disclosure of the components of an increase. Other categories require their own petitions, worksheets, notices and/or approval; see [landlord petitions](https://www.sf.gov/landlord-petitions-and-passthroughs). In particular, a mere market-rent gap is not an extraordinary circumstance under the [comparables petition instructions](https://www.sf.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/531%20Comps%20Petition%203.24.pdf).
- [California CRD](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/), [SF § 37.9](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-379-evictions) and [Civil Code § 1942.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.5) ground the nondiscrimination/retaliation analysis. Equal treatment is not a safe harbor; objectively justified differences are not automatically discriminatory. The site makes neither a finding of discrimination nor a guarantee against a claim.
- [Civil Code § 1942.4](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.4) bars rent-increase notices only when all the section’s stated conditions are met. No inference is made about this property’s condition.
- [SF § 37.10C](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3710c-use-and-sale-algorithmic-devices-prohibited) prohibits use/sale of the **defined** rental-pricing device, not all algorithms. [DOJ's 2025 proposed settlement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-requires-realpage-end-sharing-competitively-sensitive-information-and) describes allegations and a proposal, not a proven universal corporate practice; [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106643) highlights data limitations. Neither establishes a local median increase by ownership type.

## Identified source irregularities

1. The [SF general guidance](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) first example says the 1.6% rate was in effect on June 1, **2025**, although its current table and the separate announcement put 1.6% in 2026–27. Its multiple-year examples contain additional date inconsistencies. Use the [separate announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced) and [rate schedule](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit) for the rate; do not copy the conflicting dates.
2. The [banking page](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases) describes an August 2025 example, but a later sentence calls the 1.4% current allowance the rate in August **2024**. The [schedule](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit) shows 1.7% for 2024–25 and 1.4% for 2025–26. Do not repeat the mislabeled example year.
3. Some indexed SF.gov pages are archived/404 despite search snippets. Use directly opened, working links in the source register, not the archived roommate page.

## Confidentiality and evidence limits

The user’s lease details are private and owner-supplied. This public repository
contains no tenant initials, names, exact address, lease documents or individual
payment history. The data table’s property-specific values are intentionally
blank, even when an amount or date was supplied privately, because no official
record was available to verify them. This preserves rather than changes those
inputs. Placeholder unit labels are not claims of legal identity or occupancy.
Do not enter addresses or identifiable details into the site. No notice was
served, no legal professional was contacted, and no professional review is
claimed. The snapshot can go stale and must be rechecked before action.
