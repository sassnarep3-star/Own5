Leaderboard

signal and staking kept apart, with controls

Two rankings, never one. Families that publish a probability are ranked on a proper score (Brier) against the market’s own de-vigged probability, with a Holm adjustment across all nine registered families. Families that are market-state rules carry no probability at all, so ranking them on Brier was an artefact; they are ranked on hit rate against the null rate implied by the price they paid. Staking is a separate column and a separate rank, and it is never the ranking criterion.

Removal of luck

The question is not how often the model was right; it is whether it was right more often than its own probabilities said it would be. On the prospective block the model hit 53 of 81 games while its own probabilities predicted 56.27 — it was below expectation, and a hit rate of 0.6543 looks like skill only if you never compute that comparison.
