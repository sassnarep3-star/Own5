Strategies and gates

14 rules, each frozen before it saw data, each carrying the text, the windows, the gates it must clear and a two-sided statement of what would change our mind. Backtest numbers appear here and nowhere else; the leaderboard holds zero of them.

Pre-registration

The rule text, the sample windows and the gate thresholds were written into tools/season_desk_941fe39f/strategies.py before the evaluation code was run. The commit that introduced them is the registration. The windows are fixed: train 1999-2012, validation 2013-2019, holdout 2020-2025, prospective 2026.

Nothing on this page has been re-tuned after seeing a result. Where a rule could not be evaluated the reason is printed in the row, and the rule still counts in the multiplicity family, so it cannot be quietly dropped.

The eight gates

gaterulewhy it existsthresholdmeasurable here?source
G1_SAMPLEevaluation sample n >= 300 graded betsthe sample size at which a 55% edge is separable from 52.4% break-even at 80% power (luck_panel.py power calculation)300YESgithub.com
G2_PRICEevery graded bet carries a two-sided price at the same clock timeno price means no ROI; a one-sided price cannot be de-vigged (F-01 in the prior desk)two-sidedYESgithub.com
G3_CLVbeat the close: requires an opening price AND a closing price from the same book, so that CLV = (taken price - closing price) can be signedCLV is the least noisy skill measure, but it cannot be computed from a single snapshot. Measuring 'model minus closing line' instead would be circular: it is the selection rule itselftwo prices per marketNOgithub.com
G3B_ALPHAout-of-sample residual alpha: corr(model_margin - market_spread, result - market_spread) > 0 with a 95% interval excluding 0 on the holdoutthis is the only skill statement a one-snapshot corpus can support: does our disagreement with the close contain information about the result relative to the close0.0YESgithub.com
G4_BSSBrier skill score vs the de-vigged closing market > 0 on the holdoutthe market is the benchmark that matters; beating a coin flip is not skill0.0YESgithub.com
G5_CALIBRATIONcalibration slope in [0.8, 1.25] and intercept |a| < 0.05 on the holdouta model that is right on average but wrong in the tails is not usable for staking(0.8, 1.25)YESgithub.com
G6_MULTIPLICITYHolm-adjusted p < 0.05 across every strategy tested in this family settesting 12 rules at alpha 0.05 finds one 'winner' 46% of the time by chance alone0.05YESgithub.com
G7_SURVIVALBrier skill on the validation window has the same sign as on the holdouta rule that flips sign out of sample is noisesame-signYESgithub.com
G8_DECAYno single season contributes more than 40% of a strategy's total profitone lucky season is not a strategy0.4YESgithub.com

G3 (beat the close) needs an opening price and cannot be computed from one snapshot, so it fails by construction and is recorded as failed rather than skipped. G3B is the measurable substitute: does our disagreement with the close predict the result relative to the close.

Why no strategy is certified

  1. G4 (Brier skill vs the close) fails for every family: the market is better.
  2. G3B (residual alpha) fails for every family: our disagreements do not predict results out of sample.
  3. G3 (beat the close) cannot be evaluated at all: there is no opening price.
  4. G6 (multiplicity) fails: with 12 families tested, no raw p-value survives Holm.
  5. G5 (calibration) fails for most: the spread probabilities are not well calibrated at the tails.

This is not a bug in the gates. A gate that passed any of the above on this evidence would be a gate that is too weak.
