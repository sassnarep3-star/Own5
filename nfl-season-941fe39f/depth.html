Depth and replacements

Depth comes from the clubs' own charts, never from jersey order, salary or experience. A rank-1 cell holding two players is stored as a group. A cell the club left empty stays empty. The replacement is published with his depth rank, because "somebody replaced him" is not a measurement.

Shared slots, stored as groups

Seattle publishes Cooper Kupp and Rashid Shaheed in one rank-1 cell. A parser that takes "the first name in the first cell" would fabricate a single starter the club never named and silently drop the other player. This desk stores the group, counts it, and shows the count.

A group of two is a statement by the club that it has not named a single starter. The deficiency matrix treats both players as available starters rather than picking one.

Empty slots, left empty

Across the 700 carried-forward slots, two clubs publish an empty rank-1 cell (the Jets at DL and the Eagles at DT). In the five clubs re-read this session, no offence or defence row has an empty rank-1 cell; the brief's report of an empty Washington slot does not reproduce on today's page (F-07).

An empty rank-1 cell is never filled by promoting rank 2. It is stored as empty, and the club is recorded as having named nobody.

What is missing, and why it matters

Depth rank is not playing time. The brief is explicit that depth must not be modelled from jersey order, and this desk does not; but club-chart order is still an opinion. The measurable alternative is official snap counts, and the public snap-count files sit behind a host this sandbox cannot reach (F-05). Until then the honest statement is: the club named this player at this rank on this day, not this player started.
