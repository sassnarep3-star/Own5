Expected wins use the Pythagorean form with exponent 2.37 — a convention, not a league value, applied identically to every club and flagged F-11. Luck is actual minus expected, in wins. Positive means the record is better than the scoring margin explains.

How to read the table

Denver at 7-2 with a 0.537 expected rate is the clearest case: the record is ahead of the scoring. That is not proof of luck; it is a warning that the record alone overstates the team. Cleveland at 6-4 with a 0.408 expected rate is the reverse: the scoring says the record understates them.

Neither statement is a pick. Both are the kind of input a side market should already contain, and the fact that the closing line already contains it is the reason the model struggles: the luck was priced before we looked.

The same arithmetic for a strategy

The brief asks to remove luck from the strategy side too, and the desk does it three ways:

  • A cluster bootstrap on every interval, resampling whole weeks rather than rows, because games in a week share a slate and bets in a game share a result.
  • Holm adjustment across every family tested, so the number of trials is priced in.
  • A break-even test against the prices actually taken, not against 50%.

The power calculation is the useful number for planning. A bettor who truly wins 55% of -110 bets needs 2,243 graded bets to separate that from the 52.4% break-even at 80% power. A 200-bet season at 55% happens by chance 20.8% of the time for a break-even bettor. Any leaderboard shorter than that is a lottery, which is why this one is empty rather than short.

Controls as the luck floor

Three controls run on every leaderboard pass: a seeded coin flip, always-favourite and always-home. A strategy that cannot beat them is not measuring skill. On the holdout the three controls return -6.2%, -6.3% and -4.3% — the vig, as they must.
