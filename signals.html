Signal Research — Generating Useful Signals (T4)

A signal is any measurable, officially-sourced input that can move our own line estimate away from the market line. Signals are only useful if they are (a) sourced from official data, (b) fresh, and (c) validated through the gates on the Validation ↗ page. Anything that cannot be sourced officially is excluded or flagged — never estimated.

How signals become a line

1. Collect official inputs only: standings/stats nfl.com/standings ↗, nfl.com/stats ↗, injury report nfl.com/injuries ↗, schedule/byes nfl.com/schedules ↗, NGS operations.nfl.com ↗, stat corrections fantasy.nfl.com ↗.

2. Build a model price per game (our own line) from those inputs.

3. Compare with the operator-published line on official sportsbook pages (FanDuel NFL ↗, DraftKings NFL ↗) — value exists only when model price and market price disagree by more than the gate threshold.

4. Size with quarter-Kelly (gate G07); never exceed correlation caps (G05, G09).

Excluded by rule: public betting percentages, "sharp money" claims, historical closing lines, weather values per game (no structured official field), sportsbook hold % — all flagged on the Flagged for Review ↗ page, all left blank in the data tables.
