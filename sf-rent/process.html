Notice & safeguards

Use this checklist with counsel and the actual lease. This is not a completed notice or a substitute for legal service.

TopicFinding / statusEvidence
1. Confirm eligibilityCheck coverage, lawful base rent, full rent history, anniversary, existing lease terms, subsidies and prior notices. Obtain the current rent increase license before imposing an annual or banked increase.Official source ↗
2. CalculateUse the rate effective on the increase date and base rent only; distinguish annual and banked components. Avoid rounding up.Official source ↗
3. Serve written noticeAt least 30 days for increases of 10% or less, or 90 days if the increase alone or combined with other increases in the prior year exceeds 10%; add five days if mailed. Confirm method and service rules with counsel.Official source ↗
4. Explain the increaseInclude dollar amount, percentage, effective date and annual versus banked portions; identify dates underlying any banked increase. Preserve proof of service.Official source ↗
5. State a truthful basisThe annual allowance is intended to cover ordinary operating and maintenance expense increases; do not claim a particular cost as a separate authorized charge without required procedure.Official source ↗
6. Separate special chargesCapital improvement and other passthroughs have distinct procedures and possible hardship protections; do not add them as ordinary annual increases.Official source ↗
7. Review fairlyUse consistent objective criteria, check for discriminatory effects and retaliation, document the actual rationale and allow questions. Same increases do not immunize a decision.Official source ↗
8. Tenant reviewTenants can challenge unlawful increases at the Rent Board. City-funded right-to-counsel assistance concerns eviction and eligibility; it is not a guarantee of free representation for every rent-increase dispute.Official source ↗
